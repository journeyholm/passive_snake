Who is Noodle?

Noodle is the laziest snake alive. Things never get done by him — they just were done. Steer him to eat the words of each sentence in the right order and he turns active. Stop steering and he falls asleep.

How to Play

1
A passive sentence appears at the top of the grid. Passive voice hides the agent behind the action:
The window was broken by Jake. — the sentence starts with the window, not with Jake.
2
The words of the active rewrite are scattered across the grid. Steer the snake and eat them in order. Each word is color-coded by its job, and your snake keeps those colors as it grows:
Jake → broke → the window.
SUBJECT does it · VERB is the action · OBJECT gets it
Right word, wrong order still counts as a miss — word order is what makes a sentence active.
3
Later grids add distractor words — leftovers from the passive version like was, by, and past participles like broken. Eat one and you lose points, length, and a moment of speed.
4
The Zombie Test. If you can drop “by zombies” right after the verb and the sentence still works, it is passive:
The report was written [by zombies]. → works → PASSIVE
Maria wrote [by zombies] the report. → nonsense → ACTIVE
In Zombie Test levels, drive into the ACTIVE or PASSIVE gate.
5
Judgment Calls. Passive voice is not a mistake — it is a tool. Read the context, then eat gate A or B for the version that fits the writing situation better.
💤
Noodle is lazy. That is the whole problem. Leave him alone for three seconds and he falls asleep mid-grid — you will see the z z z. Keep feeding him correct words and his MOMENTUM meter climbs:
×1 Snoozing → ×1.5 Stirring → ×2 Moving → ×3 ACTIVE VOICE
That multiplier hits your score and your BYTES. Wrong words knock it straight back down. He will also complain the entire time — in passive voice, obviously — and the game will keep correcting him for you.
7
Skill Drills. Miss the same kind of thing three times in one mission and the grid pauses for a two-question drill on exactly that skill. Clear it and you are back in play with BYTES in hand.
8
Walls wrap around by default, so you cannot die on a wall. You lose a life by biting your own tail. Score becomes XP (rank) and BYTES (spending) — rank unlocks the better gear.

Three Traps To Remember

⚠
Past tense is not passive. “She ran to the store” is past tense and fully active — she is right there doing the running.
⚠
Not every “to be” is passive. Passive needs to be + a past participle. “He is tall” and “They were playing” are both active.
⚠
Passive is not always wrong. Lab reports, news about unknown suspects, and suspenseful fiction all use passive on purpose.

Settings Reference

Timed Mode gives every mission a countdown scaled to its length. Off for practice days, on for assessment days.

Skill Drills interrupt play with a two-question re-teach after a student misses the same skill three times in one mission. Drill counts are logged and exported.

Challenge Tiers. Students choose a tier, not a grade — an older student reading below grade level should never be handed a card that says “Grade 6.” The mapping is: T1 Field Notes = Gr 6 · T2 Beat Report = Gr 7 · T3 Newsroom = Gr 8 · T4 Investigation = Gr 9 · T5 Front Page = Gr 10. Every dashboard row, CSV export and answer key carries both.

Lock Tier freezes the tier selector so students play the tier you assign.

Lock Speed does the same for Slow / Medium / Fast. Challenge tier sets how hard the sentences are; game speed only sets how fast the grid moves, so Slow is a fair accommodation at any tier. The speed used is recorded on every row of the CSV.

Show All Menus. By default a new player sees only their name, The Move, and Start. Tiers, speed, the shop, the lab, contracts, trophies and the rest appear as they become relevant, each announced once. Turn this on to reveal everything immediately — useful when you are demonstrating the game to a class.

Typed Answers. The only place students type is Sentence Forge — 5 of the 57 graded questions in a tier. Turn this off and those become tap-to-build: the words appear as buttons (plus two that do not belong) and the student taps them into order. Same skill, no keyboard. Useful for tablets, shared devices, and students with motor or spelling difficulties.

Saved players. Each name entered on the home screen gets its own saved profile on this device — separate progress, BYTES, upgrades and display settings. The class log below is shared across all of them, and so are the teacher toggles on this screen, so a rotation through one Chromebook keeps everyone's work separate without you re-setting anything.

Scaffolding controls how much help the board gives before a student commits. On Auto it fades as the tier rises: Field Notes colour-codes every word by its job and shows letter-length blanks; Beat Report and Newsroom keep the role shapes but drop the colour and the length cues; Investigation and Front Page give only the passive sentence and a bank of words. Feedback after a move stays full strength at every tier.

Spiral Warm-Up remembers every item a student answers wrong and serves up to three of them back as a short warm-up before their next mission. Warm-up items are added dynamically and are not printed in the answer key. Answering one correctly retires it.

Placement Check is a 15-question pre-test (three questions per tier, no snake, no timer). Offer suggests it every time a student presses Start and lets them skip; Required removes the skip until the student has taken it once; Off hides it. Scores map to a starting tier: 0–5 → T1, 6–8 → T2, 9–11 → T3, 12–13 → T4, 14–15 → T5. Speed is suggested from accuracy and the median time per question: under 50% or 16+ seconds → Slow; 80%+ in 8 seconds or less → Fast; otherwise Medium. With Auto-place on, both are applied automatically unless Lock Tier or Lock Speed is on.

Final Check is a parallel post-test: new sentences, the same skill in the same position, so growth is a fair comparison. It stays closed until you open it. Students can take either check as many times as they like, and every attempt starts fresh from question 1. Pressing Start always offers three choices: take the Placement Check, skip it and begin at Mission 1, or continue where that player left off. The Snapshot shows the latest Placement score (it sets the tier), the best Final score, the number of attempts, and growth from the first Placement score.

Prep Pause holds the board still before every round so students can read the sentence, see the words, and plan a route before anything moves. Turn it off for timed assessments.
